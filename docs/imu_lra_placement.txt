IMU and LRA Placement Recommendations for Tremor Monitoring and Feedback System

Based on tremor analysis (Cohen's d effect sizes) and ergonomic/haptic feedback principles:

IMU Placement (for sensing tremor):
1. Dorsum of the hand, close to the wrist
   - Why: Captures global hand motion and wrist tremor; minimal soft tissue artifact; allows wrist flexion/extension.
   - Specific: Just proximal to the wrist joint on the dorsal side.

2. Thenar eminence (muscle base of the thumb)
   - Why: Sensitive to tremor in thumb abduction/adduction and opposition movements; conforms to curved, fleshy area.
   - Specific: Volar surface, at the prominence of the thumb muscles.

3. Proximal phalanx of the thumb
   - Why: Directly measures tremor at the thumb IP joint, which showed the highest effect size across diagnoses (THUMB_IP: avg effect size = 0.568 vs diagnosis 0).
   - Specific: Dorsal or lateral aspect of the thumb's proximal phalanx (between CMC and MCP joints).

LRA Placement (for haptic feedback):
- Goal: Provide perceptible vibratory feedback without inducing noise in IMU signals or interfering with hand function.
- Recommended: Dorsum of the hand, distal to the wrist IMU (e.g., on the back of the hand between the second and third metacarpals, or proximal to the fingers).
  - Why: 
    - Less sensitive than palmar side, so vibrations are easily felt without being uncomfortable.
    - Located away from the IMUs on the thenar eminence and thumb, reducing vibrational coupling.
    - Still on the dorsum, so it does not interfere with palmar grasp or fine finger movements.
- Alternative (if palmar feedback is desired): 
  - Place on the palmar side, opposite the thenar eminence (e.g., near the hypothenar eminence or central palm) but ensure it is not too close to any IMU to avoid noise.
  - Note: Palmer placement may affect grip and is more sensitive; use lower amplitude if chosen.

General Guidelines:
- Orient all IMUs consistently (e.g., Z-axis upward, X-axis radial, Y-axis proximal) to simplify data fusion.
- Secure IMUs with hypoallergenic, breathable materials (e.g., 3M Micropore tape for initial prototypes, Hypafix or silicone straps for longer wear).
- For the LRA, use a thin, flexible mount or adhesive that allows firm coupling to the skin without damping the vibration excessively.
- Ensure the LRA is driven at its resonant frequency (typically around 150-250 Hz) for efficient haptic feedback.
- Test for perceptual threshold and comfort; avoid prolonged high-amplitude vibration that could cause fatigue or skin irritation.

Summary:
- IMUs: Dorsal wrist, thenar eminence, thumb proximal phalanx.
- LRA: Dorsum of hand (distal to wrist IMU) for optimal balance of perceptibility, minimal noise coupling, and unimpeded hand function.

These placements leverage the biomechanical insights from the tremor data while respecting ergonomic and haptic design principles.
